## The stories, with the key facts as reported

1. **Kerch Bridge (lead).** Ukraine severely damaged the bridge linking
   Russia to Crimea; images shared by Ukrainian officials show a section
   of the Kerch Bridge collapsed in the water. President Zelenskyy hailed
   "an unprecedented operation" weeks in the making; a Kremlin
   spokesperson said the bridge would be rebuilt "better than before."
   Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko said the capital's Northern and Southern
   bridges were closed after Russia targeted Kyiv overnight.
   (BBC News, via Just Security; Open Chronicle adds the strikes ran a
   second consecutive day and the bridge was closed.)
2. **Iran drones at Bahrain.** Iran fired drones at a U.S. military radar
   system at the Sheikh Isa base in Bahrain; Bahrain said it intercepted
   the attack, with no reports of casualties or damage. Separately, U.S.
   officials told CNN that new intelligence indicates Iran is developing
   plans to target senior Trump administration officials with a "dirty
   bomb" or chemical weapon in Washington; the White House said there
   are no credible threats to the homeland. (Reuters and CNN, via Just
   Security.)
3. **Shenzhen summit.** President Trump travelled to Asia for the APEC
   leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, with a trilateral meeting with China's
   Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin set for Thursday 19 November
   on nuclear risks, sanctions and Ukraine; U.S. envoys Witkoff and
   Kushner meet Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin the same day.
   Trump also said he might meet North Korea's Kim Jong Un in China this
   week. (FeedCast / Hong Kong Free Press; Just Security; Open
   Chronicle.)
4. **Strait of Hormuz.** Commercial tankers were struck by naval mines
   in the Strait of Hormuz, alongside a drone alert at an Iraqi gas
   field. Brent crude rose about 0.7% to more than $93 a barrel.
   (FeedCast; Investopedia.)
5. **Markets.** U.S. stocks fell: Dow -846.25 points (-1.64%) to 50,761,
   S&P 500 -1.02% to 7,340, Nasdaq -0.74%; the S&P 500 closed below its
   200-day moving average for the first time in seven months, all 11
   sectors declined, and the VIX spiked toward 27, ahead of Nvidia's
   third-quarter earnings after the close. October retail sales rose
   0.2%, in line with forecasts. (eOption / CNBC closing data;
   Investopedia.)
6. **Japan airspace.** Japan launched fighter jets and "strongly"
   protested after two suspected Russian aircraft violated its airspace
   early in the morning; South Korea also deployed fighter jets and
   Taiwan "responded" to Russian aircraft in their air defence zones.
   (BBC News, via Just Security.)
7. **Brief — Europe / Rwanda.** Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and
   the Netherlands unveiled a joint plan for an offshore return facility
   in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers. (FeedCast.)
8. **Brief — West Bank.** Palestinian officials and witnesses said
   Israeli settlers killed a 72-year-old woman, Fadha Wadha, overnight in
   Jericho; the U.K. announced sanctions on eight Israeli construction
   companies involved in building West Bank settlements and on an
   activist group that prevents aid delivery to Gaza. (Reuters, via Just
   Security.)
9. **Brief — Geneva.** Delegates of 128 states met at the U.N. in Geneva
   for the weeklong Certain Conventional Weapons conference to decide
   whether to draft a treaty banning lethal autonomous weapons.
   (New York Times / NPR, via Just Security.)
10. **Brief — Mongolia.** President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa said he will
    resign after being accused of receiving $25 million in connection
    with the Tavantolgoi-Gashuunsukhait railway project; he rejected the
    accusations. (AP News, via Just Security.)
